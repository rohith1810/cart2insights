## Insights

### 1. Sales are concentrated in a few states

- **Observation:** The top 3 states (SP, RJ, MG) generate 62.5% of revenue.
- **Interpretation:** Demand follows population and income centres in the south-east.
- **Business impact:** Marketing spend and warehouse capacity give the best return in these states; other states are a growth opportunity.

### 2. Very few customers come back

- **Observation:** Only 3.0% of customers placed more than one order.
- **Interpretation:** The platform depends on winning new customers for almost every sale.
- **Business impact:** A retention programme (follow-up emails, loyalty discounts) could lift revenue without raising acquisition cost.

### 3. A small group of sellers earns most revenue

- **Observation:** The top 10% of sellers earn 67.5% of item revenue.
- **Interpretation:** Seller revenue is highly concentrated.
- **Business impact:** Losing a top seller is a real risk; key-account support for these sellers and onboarding help for small sellers would spread the risk.

### 4. Three categories lead item revenue

- **Observation:** health beauty, watches gifts, bed bath table make up 33.9% of revenue among the top 15 categories.
- **Interpretation:** Spending differs by category: watches gifts orders average 232.61 BRL vs 94.33 for telephony. Category explains 2.7% of the variation in order value, so pricing and promotions should be planned per category.
- **Business impact:** Stock, promotions and seller recruitment should prioritise these categories.

### 5. 6.8% of deliveries arrive late

- **Observation:** 6.8% of delivered orders arrived after the estimated date; average delivery takes 12.6 days against an average estimate of 23.7.
- **Interpretation:** Estimates are usually generous, so the late orders are real failures rather than tight promises.
- **Business impact:** Every late order is a likely low review (see next insight).

### 6. Late delivery is the clearest driver of bad reviews

- **Observation:** On-time orders average 4.29 stars; delayed orders average 2.27, and 62.4% of delayed orders get 1-2 stars.
- **Interpretation:** Delayed orders average 2.27 stars vs 4.29 for on-time orders (effect size d = 1.71). Delivery delay is linked to significantly lower satisfaction, so reducing delays should raise ratings. (t-test decision: Reject H0).
- **Business impact:** Improving delivery reliability is the most direct lever on customer satisfaction.

### 7. Remote states wait longest

- **Observation:** RR has the longest average delivery (29.4 days) with 12.2% of orders late.
- **Interpretation:** Distance from the seller hubs drives delivery time and delay.
- **Business impact:** Regional carriers or local fulfilment in these states would cut delays.

### 8. Payment method and order outcome

- **Observation:** Chi-square decision: Reject H0 (p-value < 0.0001, Cramer's V 0.0279).
- **Interpretation:** Order outcome depends on payment method. voucher has the highest cancel/unavailable rate (3.62%). Cramer's V = 0.028, so the association is weak; follow up on slow or failed payments for that method.
- **Business impact:** Where the association is real but weak, payment method is a secondary factor compared with delivery.

## Recommendations

1. Cut late deliveries: track seller dispatch time and warn or penalise sellers who ship late.
2. Tighten delivery estimates per state so customers get realistic promises.
3. Launch a second-purchase campaign (discount or free shipping) within 30 days of the first order.
4. Give the top 10% of sellers dedicated support, and help small sellers grow to reduce concentration.
5. Focus marketing budget on the leading categories and states, and test growth campaigns elsewhere.
6. Contact customers after a delayed delivery to recover the experience before they leave a review.
